# Orbits, atmospheres, missions and impacts

Methodology, sources and limitations for two linked features:

1. **Atmosphere-aware satellites, missions and launch infrastructure** (Data → *Orbits & missions*; Earth's planet view).
2. **Solar System Close Approach & Impact Scenario Lab** (Data → *Impact Lab*), and the impact events the engine rolls.

> **This is an educational and strategic simulation. It is not an operational spaceflight, collision-avoidance, reentry-safety or planetary-defense tool.** The same notice is on the page, in both desk tabs and at the head of every export.

Code: `src/data/` (snapshot, adapters, registries, provenance), `src/orbit/` (thermosphere, space weather, decay, lifecycle, SGP4 display, fleet shells), `src/impact/` (bodies, flux, entry, craters, effects, exposure, scenarios, defence, catalogue, history, engine events), `src/sim/labData.ts` (worker requests), `src/ui/dataOrbits.ts`, `src/ui/dataImpact.ts`, `src/view/orbitLayers.ts`. Tests: `scripts/orbit-check.ts`, `scripts/impact-check.ts`. Data refresh: `scripts/ingest.ts`; thermosphere calibration: `scripts/msis-calibrate.ts`.

## 1. Facts versus simulations

Every object, mission, facility, close approach, probability and impact carries exactly one classification (`src/data/provenance.ts`), drawn with its own colour, line style and badge, and filterable:

| Badge | Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| HIST | Historical observation | Happened and was recorded by the cited source |
| ACTIVE | Currently active | Observed operating at the snapshot epoch |
| SCHED | Officially scheduled | On an official manifest with a target date; not flown |
| CONTRACT | Contracted or licensed | A contract, task order or licence exists; not flown or built |
| ANNOUNCED | Announced or conceptual | Stated intent; no firm date or contract implied |
| WHAT-IF | Hypothetical user scenario | A scenario the user set up |
| SIM | Procedurally simulated future event | Generated by this future's seeded model |

Plans never carry a launch time or an outcome (`isFlown`, tested). Simulated satellites are drawn as faint orange dots on the model's shells, each standing for about 20 satellites; observed objects are solid dots in an inertial frame, shown only near their element-set epochs and labelled with the time shown, never with the simulated year.

Every sourced record carries a `Provenance`: organisation, URL, retrieval timestamp, API version or signature where the source gives one, epoch where relevant, value basis (observed, reported, estimated, simulated) and a quality grade. Unknown spacecraft and impactor properties are ranges (`Ranged`: low, nominal, high, unit, basis, source), never point values.

Wikipedia was not used as a numerical source.

**Refresh policy.** The browser never calls these services (JPL documents CORS and fair-use limits; CelesTrak asks for modest polling). `node scripts/ingest.ts [--only gp,cad,…]` fetches with per-host spacing, retries with back-off on 429/5xx, stops on other errors, validates every record through the adapters (`src/data/adapters.ts`), writes each file with its provenance and SHA-256, and recomputes the snapshot id. A source that fails keeps its previous file (offline fallback). Every export header carries both ids.

## 4. Earth's atmosphere and drag

**Thermosphere** (`src/orbit/thermosphere.ts`). NRLMSIS 2.0 is the reference. A full port was out of scope, and the reference code could not be run in this environment, so the model is a compact physical one calibrated to NRLMSIS 2.0 reference outputs:

- diffusive equilibrium of N₂, O₂, O, He, Ar and H over the Bates (1959) temperature profile, integrated analytically (Walker 1965);
- Jacchia (1971) exospheric temperature: 81-day and daily F10.7, a day/night bulge peaking mid-afternoon, geomagnetic heating in Ap (Jacchia 1977 form);
- 120 km densities that vary with solar activity, helium with season;
- USSA76 below 100 km, joined smoothly to 120 km.

Nineteen coefficients are fitted by Nelder–Mead on two thirds of the 97 reference rows above 120 km (mass density plus species densities), and tested on the held-out third: **held-out rms error 0.085 dex (≈ 22%), worst 0.18 dex (×1.5)**; training 0.076 dex. The reference data stop at 500 km; above, the profile is a physical extrapolation, within 14% of USSA76 to 700 km and ×1.66 at 1,000 km (hydrogen and helium are the least constrained). With `pymsis` installed, `scripts/msis-calibrate.ts` will refit to any denser MSIS table in the same format.

**Drag** (`src/orbit/decay.ts`), near-circular orbits:

- drag deceleration ½ ρ v_rel² / B, ballistic coefficient **B = m / (C_D A)** (kg/m²), carried explicitly, with the attitude (normal, low-drag "edge-on", tumbling) setting A;
- velocity relative to the co-rotating atmosphere, factor F = (1 − ω_E r cos i / v)²;
- da/dt = −√(μa) ρ F / B (King-Hele 1987); continuous thrust raises a at 2√(a³/μ)·T/m; propellant ṁ = T/(I_sp g₀); stationkeeping Δv = (v/2a)·Δa;
- an eccentric insertion orbit is treated at its circular-equivalent drag altitude, from the King-Hele orbit-averaged density ρ̄ = ρ_p e⁻ˣ I₀(x), x = ae/H;
- no drag above 1,500 km (MEO, GEO): their stationkeeping fights lunisolar and radiation-pressure perturbations, acknowledged and not modelled.

Orbit-mean density comes from a cached grid of the calibrated model (altitude × 81-day F10.7 × Ap × inclination band, trilinear in log ρ); the daily F10.7 departure enters through the exospheric-temperature weights.

**Lifecycle.** deployment → commissioning (low-drag) → orbit raising (thrust-limited) → operations with deadband stationkeeping → degraded propulsion or fuel exhaustion → disposal (propellant reserve, success drawn from the class prior) → natural decay (tumbling) → reentry interface (120 km) → demise with the class's surviving-mass fraction. An optional safe mode (edge-on, no thrust while Ap ≥ 20) reproduces the operator response of February 2022. Breakup and demise are a single surviving-mass fraction per class: this is not an ORSAT or DAS analysis.

**Class priors** (`src/orbit/classes.ts`): Starlink v1.x and v2 mini, Amazon Leo, OneWeb, ISS, HST, CubeSat, unknown LEO, debris, MEO/GEO. Each value is a low/nominal/high range marked reported, estimated or assumed; most propulsion and all unit costs are design assumptions. Observed objects are mapped to a class by name and catalogue number, which is itself labelled as an inference.

**Lifetimes and ranges** (`src/orbit/lifetime.ts`): seeded Monte Carlo over the class prior (triangular draws), reporting 10th/50th/90th percentiles of natural decay, powered life, stationkeeping Δv and propellant per year. Broad checks against NASA's orbital-debris guidance: B = 50 kg/m² decays within a year or two from 400 km and persists for centuries from 800 km under the seeded Sun.

**SGP4 display** (`src/orbit/sgp4.ts`): satellite.js 7.1 `json2satrec` on OMM records, nothing added. The fitted elements already contain the drag each object felt; adding the decay model on top would count drag twice. `sgp4.ts` imports no drag or atmosphere module (tested), and display positions equal satellite.js output exactly (tested). Element age decides what is drawn: ≤ 3 days fresh; 3–14 days approximate; 14–60 days illustrative; beyond 60 days not drawn at all.

**Tiered fidelity.** SGP4 for observed positions (worker, on request); aggregate shells for thousands of simulated satellites; full lifecycle integration only for one selected object, one class, the storm-loss table and the validation case; cached density grids; everything expensive in the existing Web Worker.

## 5. Space weather

- **Observed** (GFZ daily Ap/F10.7, NOAA SWPC): conditions now, and the February 2022 validation window.
- **Modes**: quiet (F10.7 70, Ap 4), nominal (the seeded cycle and observed daily statistics), disturbed (declining-phase, high-activity Ap distribution with storms four times as frequent).
- **Deep future** (`src/orbit/spaceWeather.ts`): cycles 26 onward bootstrapped from the 24 complete recorded cycles (amplitude, length and rise kept together, so the Waldmeier relation survives), ±10% jitter, Hathaway, Wilson & Reichmann (1994) shape; cycle 25 fitted to its observed peak (smoothed SSN 160.9, late 2024) and latest decline; grand minima at the Usoskin et al. (2007) long-run rate (≈ 27 in 11,400 years, 3–8 cycles long, residual 12% amplitude). F10.7 from the observed monthly relation F10.7 = 63.0 + 0.745·SSN (residual 4.9 sfu), daily lognormal scatter 0.128 with 27-day rotation. Daily Ap from the observed quantiles for the same 81-day SN bin and cycle phase; storms (Ap ≥ 30) as runs with the observed persistence (0.41) and the observed tail above 30/50/100/200. Checked: generated P(Ap ≥ 50) = 2.0% against 2.2% observed over the last two years.

A present-day NOAA forecast is never extended into the deep future.

## 6. Validation: the February 2022 Starlink loss

49 satellites deployed on 2022-02-03 into a 210 × ≈320 km insertion orbit; observed daily Ap 26 and 32 on 3–4 February (GFZ); 38 of 49 lost (Fang et al. 2022). Model (49 draws from the Starlink v1.x prior, safe mode while Ap ≥ 20, then raising to 550 km):

| Run | Lost |
|---|---|
| Observed | 78% |
| Calibrated model, observed daily Ap | 12% |
| Storm days raised to +50% over pre-storm (Fang et al., WAM-IPE low end) | 29% |
| Storm days raised to +125% (high end) | 59% |
| Same launch, quiet conditions | 0% |
| +125%, insertion perigee 250 or 300 km | 0% |

The qualitative outcome is reproduced: a low insertion orbit plus storm-enhanced density gives heavy losses; quiet conditions or a higher insertion give none. The empirical model alone underestimates the storm's density response (+21% at 250 km), as Fang et al. found for NRLMSISE-00 in the pre-launch analysis; their physics-based enhancement closes most of the gap. The model was not tuned to hit 78%.

## 7. Impacts

### 7.1 Three event systems, never mixed

1. **Observed historical impacts** (`src/impact/history.ts`): Chelyabinsk, Tunguska, Meteor Crater, Shoemaker–Levy 9, Jupiter 2009, Mars 2021-12-24, the Moon 2013-03-17, 2008 TC3. Never rolled by the engine; used for validation.
2. **Catalogued close approaches and official risk**: JPL CAD for every body; Sentry for Earth. Inside the simulation each Sentry virtual impactor from 2040 onwards is rolled once with its official probability, as a known object (decades of warning).
3. **Seeded background impacts** for the deep future, at each body's own rate. On Earth, before the catalogue horizon (2126, about a century after retrieval), each background draw belongs to the already-catalogued population with probability equal to the 2026 completeness for its size and is dropped, so a known object is never counted twice (tested). After the horizon the catalogue predicts nothing, and the background keeps everything; completeness then only affects warning.

Current catalogue objects are not propagated for ten thousand years.

### 7.2 Close approaches are not probabilities

A close approach is a nominal miss distance with 3-sigma bounds. The lab never converts distance, 3-sigma minimum or diameter into a probability. Earth shows Sentry's cumulative probability, Palermo and Torino values, with the note that they change as observations arrive. Other bodies show "No official impact probability available" unless a named authority published one: the only such record is NASA's 2025 assessment of a ≈ 4% lunar impact by 2024 YR4 in 2032, shown as **superseded**, since JWST astrometry in February 2026 ruled it out (NASA, 2026-03-05). A derived probability would need orbit covariance, virtual-impactor sampling and n-body propagation with planetary ephemerides; none is implemented, so none is shown.

### 7.3 Pipeline

`src/impact/scenario.ts`: object properties with uncertainty (diameter in log space; composition stony 60%, carbonaceous 30%, iron 5%, cometary 5% for the inner system, cometary for the giants' side; bulk density by composition; strength from the Collins et al. law ×10 each way, after Popova et al. 2011) → target body profile → impact speed √(v∞² + v_esc²) (gravitational focusing also raises the rate by 1 + v_esc²/v∞²) → entry angle from P(θ) ∝ sin 2θ → atmosphere or none → break-up, airburst, deep deposition or ground impact → effects by class → spatial exposure → 10th/50th/90th percentiles of a seeded Monte Carlo.

### 7.4 Physics by body class

**Entry** (`entry.ts`): one-dimensional fragmentation model after Chyba et al. 1993 and Hills & Goda 1993 with the Collins et al. 2005 strength law and dispersion criterion (f_p = 7), ablation σ = 0.014 s²/km², straight-line flight, run through each body's own density profile. Not NASA ATAP-grade physics.

**Rocky with atmospheres** (Venus, Earth, Mars, Titan): airburst or ground impact; crater if material survives; blast from Collins et al. 2005 eqs. 54–57, Sachs-scaled to the body's surface pressure (weak on Mars, overwhelming on Venus); thermal radiation on Earth only (the fireball model is calibrated for Earth air); ejecta; seismic shaking; tsunami only on Earth's ocean (Ward & Asphaug 2000 1/r decay, uncertain ×10); global dust and climate on Earth above ≈ 10⁵ Mt (Toon et al. 1997).

**Airless** (Mercury, the Moon, small moons, asteroids): full-speed impact; π-scaling crater (Holsapple / Richardson 2009, Cratermaker constants) with the Schenk et al. (2021) gravity-dependent transition; ejecta blanket t = D_tc⁴/(112 r³); seismic magnitude 0.67 log₁₀E − 5.87 (Earth attenuation, noted); ejecta faster than orbital and escape speed (Housen & Holsapple 2011). No blast, no tsunami, no airburst (tested).

**Giants** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune): altitude relative to 1 bar; isothermal above and dry adiabat below; energy-deposition depth from the entry model; plume height and scar size scaled from Shoemaker–Levy 9 (scar ∝ E^⅓ anchored at 12,000 km for 6 million Mt, plume weakly energy-dependent near 3,000 km, duration ∝ E^⅓ from about a month); shock-chemistry signatures listed. **No crater and no ground casualties are ever computed** (tested).

Ice crater constants: the Cratermaker "Ice" constant (K1 = 15.6) gave an 8.8 km crater for a 100 m comet on Europa and is not used; cold outer-system ice is treated as a competent non-porous solid (μ = 0.55, ≈ 1 MPa, ice density): a labelled design choice.

### 7.6 Exposure, warning and mitigation

Settlements are spatial (`exposure.ts`): sites at the same places the 3D view draws its cities (`cityDir`, `cityCount`, the capital's 30% share), footprints from people and stage density (a design assumption), capital spread over three times the area. Damage is integrated exactly over each footprint (circle–circle overlap for each band of the piecewise-constant damage curve). Orbital habitats are not hit by surface effects; on airless worlds, impacts lofting ≥ 10⁹ kg above the habitats are checked with a one-crossing fluence of ≥ 10 cm fragments against shielded habitats (assumption). Earth's geography is not mapped in this simulation, so Earth's exposure is statistical but still area-based: land or ocean, urban (≈ 1% of land, ≈ 57% of people) or rural density, coastline density for tsunamis.

Detection, warning and deflection (`defense.ts`, design assumptions, all labelled): detection with the survey completeness for the size and year, lower away from Earth; known objects give 5–60 years of warning; deflection succeeds 90% of the time within the era's capability (≈ 300 m with DART-class impactors from 2040, ≈ 2 km with in-space industry, ≈ 20 km with fusion propulsion); otherwise evacuation cuts deaths to 5% of the unwarned toll. Defenders act only on impacts their surveys predict would do harm.

## 8. Economics coupling

`src/orbit/shells.ts`: Earth's simulated fleet is six aggregate shells (VLEO 350 km, broadband LEO 550 and 630 km, high LEO 1,200 km, navigation MEO, GEO). Fleet size is a design assumption (40,000 in 2040, range 20,000–80,000, growing with √(Earth product)). Each step, the drag at each shell's altitude under the seeded Sun sets stationkeeping Δv and propellant; propellant sets the operating life (with a 10% disposal reserve); life sets replacements; storms during the 30-day raising window take the share the cached decay calculation predicts; a Kessler cascade costs about 2% of LEO assets a year (assumption). Replacements are paid at the class unit cost (assumption) plus mass × the launch-economics model's Earth-to-orbit cost per kg, and charged to Earth's capital. New metrics: satellites in service, replacements, spending, storm losses, availability, propellant, F10.7, sunspot number, Ap, storm days, impacts, impact deaths, impacts averted.

The Orbits tab shows the altitude trade (cost per slot-year and operating life against altitude, in quiet, nominal and disturbed conditions). For an observed object, replacement exposure is the class unit cost plus launch at the published Falcon 9 list price per kg ($69.75M over 15–22.8 t): a list price, not a cost. Mission contract values appear only where NASA published them (TESS, Psyche, Europa Clipper, Dragonfly), with their scope; DART has none because no official figure was checked.

## 10. Known limitations

- The thermosphere is a calibrated compact model, not NRLMSIS 2.0; above 500 km it is unconstrained by the reference data. Geomagnetic storms respond to daily Ap, not 3-hourly; storm-time enhancement is underestimated (§6).
- Drag uses near-circular orbits; no lunisolar, radiation-pressure or J2 effects; no conjunctions or collision avoidance; breakup is one surviving fraction.
- Spacecraft properties are priors; most propulsion values and every unit cost are assumptions.
- The fleet is aggregate; it is not a constellation design.
- Entry is one-dimensional; vulnerability curves are coarse; Earth exposure is statistical; tsunami heights are uncertain ×10; large complex craters are underestimated by up to ×2.
- Giant-planet plumes and scars are SL9-scaled, not modelled; deep-atmosphere structure is a dry adiabat.
- Impact rates for Mercury, Venus, Neptune, the Kuiper belt and every other star are analogues with wide ranges.
- Detection, warning and deflection parameters are design assumptions.
- Facility coordinates are public site centres with stated precision; the procedural Earth texture does not show real coastlines, so facilities are not drawn on the globe.
- The mission registry is curated (≈ 30 flown missions, a handful of plans), not a complete manifest.

## 11. Recommended scientific improvements

1. Run NRLMSIS 2.0 directly (pymsis at build time) to replace the calibration with a full lookup table; add DTM-2020 or JB2008 for storm-time comparison; use 3-hourly ap.
2. Eccentric-orbit decay (King-Hele averaging through the decay), J2 node precession for shells, and a debris-environment model (NASA ORDEM/ESA MASTER) for collision risk.
3. Ingest the full NSSDCA and GCAT launch records for a complete mission manifest, with automated role extraction.
4. Replace the fragment model with a calibrated fragment-cascade model (ATAP-style) and validate against the CNEOS fireball database.
